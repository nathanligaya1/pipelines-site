Shot sizes

How much of the person fills the frame. Bigger face = more emotion.

tap: slow-mo

Wide shot

WS

The whole scene: the person plus the room around them.

Use it: To show where we are, or as a calm reset between tight shots. In our podcast footage the full webcam frame is the widest we have.

Ask for it like this“0:04, go wide (show the whole frame) for 2s as a reset.”
Shown here as the full 16:9 webcam frame placed in a vertical video.
tap: slow-mo

Medium shot

MS

Waist or chest up. Face and hands both visible.

Use it: The default talking shot. Good when the speaker uses their hands.

Ask for it like this“0:00 to 0:10, keep Jerome in a medium so we see his hands.”
tap: slow-mo

Medium close-up

MCU

Chest up. The face is big but you still see the shoulders.

Use it: The best everyday framing for vertical clips. Personal but not too tight.

Ask for it like this“Make the MCU the default framing for the whole clip.”
tap: slow-mo

Close-up

CU

The face fills most of the frame.

Use it: For the emotional line, the key claim, or a strong reaction.

Ask for it like this“0:18, cut to a close-up on JMac for his reaction.”
tap: slow-mo

Extreme close-up

ECU

Only part of the face: eyes and mouth.

Use it: Very rarely, for one dramatic word. Webcam footage gets soft this tight, so use it for a second at most.

Ask for it like this“0:27, ECU on Jerome's eyes for 1 beat on 'never'.”
Soft on purpose: this is what webcam footage looks like when you crop this far in.

Camera moves

We fake these with a digital zoom or crop. The camera itself never moved.

tap: slow-mo

Slow zoom / push-in

SLOW PUSH

The frame slowly moves closer to the face over a few seconds.

Use it: To build tension or pull the viewer in while someone makes an important point.

Ask for it like this“0:12, slow push-in on Jerome for 3s, subtle.”
tap: slow-mo

Pull-out

ZOOM OUT

The opposite of a push-in: we slowly move back and see more.

Use it: At the end of a thought, to let it breathe, or to reveal context.

Ask for it like this“0:40, slow pull-out over the last 3s of the clip.”
tap: slow-mo

Punch-in

INSTANT ZOOM / JUMP ZOOM

An instant zoom on a cut. No motion, the frame just jumps tighter.

Use it: On the key word or joke. The most common emphasis move in short-form.

Ask for it like this“0:09, punch in on 'million', about 30% tighter.”
tap: slow-mo

Ken Burns

PAN + ZOOM ON A PHOTO

Slow pan and zoom across a still photo so it feels alive.

Use it: Any time we show a still image: a screenshot, a property photo, a headline.

Ask for it like this“Put the property photo at 0:20 with a slow Ken Burns, left to right.”
tap: slow-mo

Whip pan

WHIP / SWISH TRANSITION

A fast blurry swipe from one shot to the next.

Use it: Energetic transitions between speakers or topics. Use it once per clip, not more.

Ask for it like this“0:15, whip pan from JMac to Jerome instead of a plain cut.”
tap: slow-mo

Handheld shake

CAMERA SHAKE

A small, random wobble that makes a locked-off shot feel like a real camera.

Use it: To add energy to a static webcam shot. Keep it subtle or it looks fake.

Ask for it like this“Add a subtle handheld shake on the Jerome close-ups.”

Cuts and transitions

How one shot changes to the next.

tap: slow-mo

Hard cut

STRAIGHT CUT

One shot ends, the next begins. No effect.

Use it: 95% of all edits. When in doubt, it is a hard cut.

Ask for it like this“Just hard cuts between speakers, no transitions.”
tap: slow-mo

Jump cut

JUMP

Cut out a piece of the same shot, so the person jumps slightly.

Use it: To remove pauses, ums and slow parts. It keeps the pace fast.

Ask for it like this“Tighten 0:05 to 0:15 with jump cuts, remove all pauses.”
Tip: a punch-in on every other jump cut hides the jump.
tap: slow-mo

J-cut

AUDIO LEADS

You hear the next person before you see them.

Use it: Makes a conversation flow. The viewer is already listening when the picture changes.

Ask for it like this“0:22, J-cut: start Jerome's voice under JMac for half a second, then cut to him.”
Our demos are silent, so the labels show what you would hear.
tap: slow-mo

L-cut

AUDIO TRAILS

The picture cuts to the listener while the speaker keeps talking.

Use it: To show a reaction while the point is still being made.

Ask for it like this“0:30, L-cut: keep Jerome's audio but show JMac nodding for 1s.”
tap: slow-mo

Match cut

MATCH ON SHAPE / POSITION

Two shots lined up so something (a face, a shape, a motion) sits in the same spot across the cut.

Use it: For a slick, 'designed' feel. Here both faces are sized and placed the same, so the cut feels smooth.

Ask for it like this“0:14, match cut from JMac to Jerome: same face size and position.”
A classic match cut matches an action (a hand raise into a hand raise). We only had faces to match.
tap: slow-mo

Cross-dissolve

DISSOLVE / CROSSFADE

One shot fades into the next, both visible for a moment.

Use it: Time passing, or a soft change of topic. Rare in punchy short-form.

Ask for it like this“0:35, cross-dissolve into the next topic, 0.5s.”
tap: slow-mo

Dip to black

FADE THROUGH BLACK

Fade out to black, then fade in to the next shot.

Use it: A clear chapter break, or before a big reveal.

Ask for it like this“0:50, dip to black before the final line.”
tap: slow-mo

Smash cut

HARD CONTRAST CUT

A sudden cut from something calm to something loud or intense.

Use it: For surprise or comedy. The contrast is the point.

Ask for it like this“0:08, smash cut from the quiet wide to a tight close-up on 'and then we lost it all'.”

Our clip vocabulary

The words we use for the podcast clips.

tap: slow-mo

Split screen

HOST TOP / GUEST BOTTOM

Both people on screen at once: JMac on top, Jerome on the bottom.

Use it: Our default layout for back-and-forth conversation.

Ask for it like this“Use split screen, host top, guest bottom, for the whole clip.”
tap: slow-mo

Full-screen takeover

PATTERN INTERRUPT

Break out of the split screen and fill the screen with one person.

Use it: On the key line. The change of layout wakes the viewer up.

Ask for it like this“0:17 to 0:20, full-screen takeover on Jerome, then back to split.”
tap: slow-mo

Freeze frame

HOLD FRAME

In split screen, the person who is not talking holds their last frame until they speak again.

Use it: Keeps attention on the speaker and hides awkward listening faces.

Ask for it like this“Freeze the non-speaker in the split; unfreeze when they talk.”
tap: slow-mo

Cold open

START WITH THE PEAK

Open on the most dramatic moment, before any intro or context.

Use it: Almost always. The first second decides if they keep watching.

Ask for it like this“Cold open with the 'we lost $2 million' line from 1:40, then start the story.”

Hook / payoff / kicker

CLIP STRUCTURE

Hook: the first 1 to 3 seconds that stop the scroll. Payoff: the moment the hook promised. Kicker: a last line or twist that makes people rewatch or comment.

Use it: Every clip. If a clip has no clear payoff, it is not ready.

Ask for it like this“Hook is 0:00 to 0:03, payoff at 0:25, end on the kicker 'and that's why I never sign first'.”
tap: slow-mo

B-roll

CUTAWAY

Footage that is not the person talking: a photo, a screen, an object. The voice continues on top.

Use it: To illustrate what they talk about, or to hide a jump cut.

Ask for it like this“0:11, cut to B-roll of the property photo for 2s while he keeps talking.”
tap: slow-mo

Lower third

NAME TAG / NAME SUPER

Name and title in the lower part of the screen.

Use it: The first time a person appears.

Ask for it like this“Add a lower third for Jerome at 0:03: 'Jerome, real estate investor'.”
tap: slow-mo

Speed ramp

SLOW-MO TO FAST

Speed changes smoothly within one shot: normal, slow, then fast.

Use it: To emphasize a gesture or a moment. Works best with movement.

Ask for it like this“0:06, speed ramp on the hand gesture: slow it down, then snap back.”
tap: slow-mo

Reframing

16:9 TO 9:16

Choose which vertical slice of the wide video to keep. The red box is what the phone sees.

Use it: Every clip we cut from the landscape podcast. The crop should follow the speaker.

Ask for it like this“Reframe to 9:16 and keep Jerome's face centered, don't cut off his hands.”

Color

How the picture looks: brightness, contrast, warmth, mood.

tap: slow-mo

Color grade

GRADE

The overall look we give the footage: contrast, warmth, saturation.

Use it: Every clip, so the footage looks polished and on brand.

Ask for it like this“Grade it a little warmer and punchier, keep skin tones natural.”
tap: slow-mo

Color match

MATCH THE CAMERAS

Make two different cameras look like they were shot in the same room.

Use it: Always with split screen. JMac's set is warm orange, Jerome's office is cool gray.

Ask for it like this“Color match JMac and Jerome so they feel like one show.”
tap: slow-mo

LUT

LOOK PRESET

A preset 'filter' file that applies a whole look in one step.

Use it: To test different moods fast. A LUT is a starting point, not the final grade.

Ask for it like this“Try a warm film LUT on this one, keep it at 50% strength.”
tap: slow-mo

Crushed vs lifted blacks

BLACK LEVEL

Crushed: the dark parts go pure black (punchy, contrasty). Lifted: the darks turn gray (soft, faded, 'film' look).

Use it: Crushed for bold and modern. Lifted for nostalgic or soft. Don't crush so far that the black shirt loses all detail.

Ask for it like this“Crush the blacks a little for more punch, but keep detail in his shirt.”
